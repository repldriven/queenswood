# Payments: gap analysis

Subject: [payments.md](../payments.md) — Payments and ClearBank
choreography.

## Verdict

**Fail.**

The shape the TDD describes is built. The three records, the two
processors and their routing, the reservation, settlement and reversal
legs, suspense for an unmatched or unopened creditor, the held inbound
lifecycle, the adapter's intent and outbox, and the simulator's
triggers are all there. Most of them have tests.

The verdict fails on five ways money is miscounted, lost or exposed
without anyone being told, none of which the TDD mentions:

- An inbound amount with pence can be credited a penny short (F1).
- A second inbound that reuses an end-to-end id is acknowledged and
  dropped before the payment brick sees it (F2).
- A settlement event that fails three times is committed past and
  lost. That includes an inbound a policy refuses, and any event
  processed during a short FDB outage (F3).
- An outbound payment can stay `pending` with its funds reserved, and
  no one is told. The relay gives up after about two seconds of
  failed POSTs, the scheme command is sent once and never again, and
  an assessment-failure webhook spelled as ClearBank documents it is
  acknowledged with no events (F4).
- Any bank can read another bank's internal or outbound payment by id
  (F5).

The verdict flips to pass when amounts are converted exactly, inbound
dedup is keyed on the scheme's transaction id, a failed settlement
event is dead-lettered and alerted, a stuck outbound is found and
resolved, payment reads are scoped to the caller's bank, and the TDD
describes each of these.

## What was examined

- The subject TDD, the payments PRD, and the TDDs it cites:
  transaction processing, transactions and balances, idempotency and
  service APIs, plus policy evaluation.
- The `payment` brick: `interface.clj`, `commands.clj`, `core.clj`,
  `domain.clj`, `events.clj`, `store.clj` and `system.clj`. The
  `payment-query` brick's interface and store.
- The `clearbank-relay` component, the `clearbank-webhook` schemas, and
  the `clearbank-adapter` base: `commands.clj`, `clearbank.clj`,
  `publisher.clj`, `system.clj`, the webhook handlers and routes, and
  the CoP routes.
- The `clearbank-simulator` base: the FPS, simulate and webhook
  handlers.
- The `api` base's payment routes, commands, queries and components,
  its `commands.clj`, and the simulate handlers.
- The payment, transaction and ClearBank protos, the payment indexes
  in
  [fdb-record-types.yml](/components/resources/resources/system/fdb-record-types.yml),
  the `payment`, `clearbank-adapter` and `clearbank-adapter-relay`
  system groups, the financial processors, external adapters and
  exclusive dispatchers `application.yml` files, the Kafka topic list,
  and the Helm values for those services.
- In the pinned `mono` (v0.0.30): the `event` processor, the Kafka and
  Pulsar consumers' redelivery, and the server's JSON decoder.
- The payment, adapter, relay and simulator tests, the payment API
  scenarios, the payment domain scenarios, and the model's transfers.

Nothing was executed except one Clojure expression, to confirm F1's
arithmetic. Every other finding was traced in source, and the ones
that predict runtime behaviour say so.

## What matches

- **The records.** InternalPayment has no status, OutboundPayment and
  InboundPayment carry the fields and statuses the TDD lists, and each
  links to its Transaction by `transaction-id`. `processing` is in the
  enum and unused, as stated.
- **The processors.** `dispatch` handles the two submit commands, and
  `dispatch-event` routes on the event and its `debit-credit-code` to
  the six handlers in the table. A rejection with no code takes the
  outbound path.
- **The outbound legs.** Submission reserves on the debtor's
  pending-outgoing and 1200. Settlement clears both and posts the
  outflow, debtor posted against 1100. Rejection releases the
  reservation. Each runs in one FDB transaction with its status flip.
  A repeated settlement or rejection is a no-op, and a rejection of a
  completed payment is refused.
- **The inbound legs.** A settled credit is deduplicated by
  `scheme-transaction-id` inside the settling transaction. A BBAN that
  matches no account, or matches one that is not opened, is parked in
  that sort code's bank's 2500, and a sort code no bank owns fails. A
  held credit moves no money, is released by `end_to_end_id` to
  1100 → creditor, or is returned with nothing posted.
- **The adapter.** A `submit-payment` becomes a pending intent and is
  acknowledged, with a redelivery deduplicated on the end-to-end id. A
  relay POSTs outside any FDB transaction. A webhook is written to the
  outbox and its changelog in one transaction, answering 200 only on
  commit and 500 otherwise. An assessment failure fans out one
  `transaction-rejected` per `EndToEndId` with `CB_AssessmentFailed`.
  The receiver authenticates nobody, as the TDD states.
- **The simulator.** It serves `/v3/payments/fps`,
  `/simulate/inbound-payment` with its `outcome`, the held sentinel
  name, sort code `000000` for assessment failure, and a configured
  webhook delay.
- **Idempotency.** Both submit routes carry the idempotency pair, and
  the store's key indexes are headed by `bank_id`.
- **The channels.** Submission, the scheme command and the scheme
  events each have their own channel on the one bus abstraction.
- **The tests.** Every transaction builder has a domain test, and key
  scoping has a store test. API scenarios cover replay, a race, a lost
  reply, two banks with one key, held then declined, assessment
  failure, and suspense for an unmatched and a closed account. Domain
  scenarios cover inbound held then released, returned or sent to a
  closed account, event redelivery, and the daily limits.

## Findings

Severity is High where money is miscounted, lost or exposed, or a
stated guarantee is broken. Medium is where the design and the code
disagree in a way a reader would act on. Low is where a document is
incomplete.

### F1. High — an inbound amount can lose a penny

Traced, with the conversion evaluated. The server decodes a JSON number
with a fraction as a double, and the adapter turns a ClearBank amount
into pence with `(long (* amount 100))`, which truncates. `0.29` becomes
`28`. Of the amounts from 0.00 to 999.99, 4,586 lose a penny this way.
The inbound handlers credit the amount the event carries, so the
customer is credited, and 1100 debited, a penny less than ClearBank
received. Outbound settlement is unaffected because it posts the
payment's stored amount. Every scenario amount is a whole number of
pounds, so no test sees it. Evidence: `amount->minor-units` in
[publisher.clj](/bases/clearbank-adapter/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/clearbank_adapter/publisher.clj).

Fix: convert through `BigDecimal` from the decoded text, rounding
half-even and refusing a value with more than two decimal places, or
decode the webhook body with big decimals. Add a scenario with a
pence amount such as 0.29.

### F2. High — an inbound that reuses an end-to-end id is dropped

The TDD says inbound dedup is by `scheme-transaction-id` and that
ClearBank does not guarantee unique inbound end-to-end ids. The adapter
deduplicates first, on `EndToEndTransactionId` plus the event kind, and
answers 200 to a duplicate. A second, distinct inbound that carries an
end-to-end id seen before is never written to the outbox, so it is
neither credited nor parked in suspense, and ClearBank is told it
arrived. A sender that has no id of its own commonly fills the field
with a placeholder, which makes a repeat likely. The same key decides
held webhooks, so a second hold with that id is dropped too. In the
payment brick, `get-held-inbound-by-end-to-end-id` reads the first
record for the id and returns nil unless that one is held, so a
repeated id can hide an open hold from its release or return.
Evidence: the inbound mappers in
[publisher.clj](/bases/clearbank-adapter/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/clearbank_adapter/publisher.clj),
and the payment-query
[store.clj](/components/payment-query/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/payment_query/store.clj).

Fix: key an inbound settlement's dedup on `TransactionId`. Key a held
webhook on the fields that identify it, and match a release or return
to the held record by end-to-end id and creditor BBAN, reading every
record for the id rather than the first. Say in the TDD what the
adapter deduplicates on.

### F3. High — a settlement event that fails three times is lost

Traced, not executed. mono's event processor rethrows an anomaly so the
bus redelivers. Kafka's consumer redelivers by seeking back, with no
delay, and after three attempts commits past the message, sending it to
a dead-letter topic only if the consumer has a dead-letter producer.
The `schemes-payments-event` consumer in the financial processors
service has none, while the command consumers beside it do. The
prediction is that the event is logged and gone in each of these
cases:

- **A policy refuses an inbound.** `settle-inbound` checks the
  receive capability, the daily count and the currency, and answers a
  rejection. The `daily-limit-breach-inbound` domain scenario asserts
  that rejection. In the running system the money has arrived at
  ClearBank and the webhook has been acknowledged, but no record,
  posting or suspense entry is made.
- **The sort code belongs to no bank.** The TDD says this "fails". It
  fails the same way.
- **FDB is unavailable** for longer than three immediate retries. A
  settlement, hold or rejection for any payment is lost, and the
  outbound it concerned stays `pending` or `held`.
- **A rejection arrives for a completed outbound**, or any event names
  an outbound payment that does not exist.

The TDD's inbound goal, "the receipt is not dropped", and its table
row "(rejected) anomaly" both leave out what happens next. Evidence:
the `schemes-payments-event` consumer in the financial processors
[application.yml](/projects/financial-processors-service/resources/application.yml),
`process` in mono's `event/processor.clj`, and `apply-ack!` in mono's
`kafka/consumer.clj`.

Fix: give the consumer a dead-letter producer and topic, and alert on
anything written to it. Never refuse money that has already arrived:
park a policy-refused inbound in suspense as an unmatched one is.
Back off between redeliveries so a short outage is ridden out. State
all of this in the TDD.

### F4. High — an outbound payment can stay pending, unnoticed

The TDD's Known Limitations name a missing webhook and a broker outage
at publish time. The code has four more ways to reach the same state,
with the customer's funds reserved and no signal but a log line:

- **The relay gives up after about two seconds.** `relay-one` retries
  every 200 ms and marks an intent `failed` after 10 attempts, and no
  configuration overrides either default. A failed intent publishes
  nothing, so the payment is never rejected and its reservation never
  released. A 4xx that will never succeed is retried the same way.
  Any status below 400 marks the intent sent without reading the
  per-instruction response in the body.
- **The scheme command is sent once.** `publish-scheme-command`
  ignores the result of `message-bus/send` and returns ACCEPTED.
  A command redelivered after a crash between the commit and the
  publish finds the payment already saved, and `submit-outbound`
  skips the publish because the save was a uniqueness violation. The
  adapter's intent dedup would make a second publish safe.
- **An assessment failure spelled as ClearBank sends it is
  acknowledged with no events.** The TDD says the adapter must
  tolerate `AssesmentFailure` before going live. The route's body
  schema is an open map, so a payload using that key maps to an empty
  list, and the handler answers 200 having written nothing.
- **The debtor's BBAN is read after the commit.** If that read fails,
  the required `debtor_bban` is nil, serialisation fails, and the
  failure is only logged, so nothing is published.

Evidence:
[outbound.clj](/components/clearbank-relay/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/clearbank_relay/outbound.clj),
`publish-scheme-command` and `submit-outbound` in the payment
[core.clj](/components/payment/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/payment/core.clj),
and `outbound-payment-assessment-failed` in
[publisher.clj](/bases/clearbank-adapter/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/clearbank_adapter/publisher.clj).

Fix: when the relay gives up, or ClearBank refuses the instruction,
publish a `transaction-rejected` so the payment fails and its funds
are released. Retry with backoff over minutes, not milliseconds.
Republish on redelivery while the payment is `pending`. Accept both
spellings of the assessment payload. Add the reconciliation sweep the
TDD defers, reporting any payment pending past a threshold.

### F5. High — any bank can read another bank's payments

The PRD's goal is "Tenants don't see each other's payments". The
OutboundPayment and InternalPayment primary keys are `payment_id`
alone. `get-outbound-payment` and `get-internal-payment` take no bank,
and the `api` handlers pass none and do not compare the record's
`bank_id` with the caller's. A principal with `org:viewer` on one bank
that holds another bank's payment id is answered 200 with the debtor
account, creditor BBAN and name, amount and reference. Party, account
and balance reads are all scoped by bank, and `two-banks-one-key` reads
each bank's payment only with its own token. Evidence: the payment
[queries.clj](/bases/api/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/api/payment/queries.clj)
and the payment-query
[interface.clj](/components/payment-query/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/payment_query/interface.clj).

Fix: take `bank-id` in both reads and answer 404 for another bank's
record. Add a scenario where bank B's token reads bank A's payment and
is answered 404.

### F6. Medium — a settlement after a rejection posts money that never left

`settle-outbound` skips a `completed` payment and settles anything
else, including a `failed` one whose reservation was already released.
It flips the payment to `completed` and posts the settlement legs,
clearing the debtor's pending-outgoing and 1200 a second time and
debiting the debtor's posted balance. The transaction's idempotency
keys, `settle-out-` and `reverse-out-`, differ, so nothing stops it.
The lifecycle-transitions recipe says an event handler gates on the
expected source state. Here that would be `pending` or `held`. The
TDD's outbound table has no row for `failed` + `transaction-settled`.
Out-of-order delivery is the TDD's own stated risk, and the webhook
receiver is unauthenticated. Evidence: `settle-outbound` in the payment
[events.clj](/components/payment/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/payment/events.clj).

Fix: settle only `pending` or `held`. Log and skip `failed`, with an
alert, since it means the scheme and the books disagree. Add the row
to the table and a domain scenario.

### F7. Medium — the adapter can only reach the simulator

The TDD says the adapter "calls the real ClearBank FPS API" and the
simulator is "not deployed in production". The code says otherwise:

- In every non-test profile, the outbound runner's `clearbank-url` is
  `CLEARBANK_SIMULATOR_URL`.
- The registrar registers the webhooks by POSTing to the simulator.
- The POST carries only a content type, with no credential or request
  signature configured anywhere.
- The external adapters service includes the simulator's server group
  in its production `application.yml`, and the Helm values point the
  adapter at the simulator on `localhost`.

That is consistent with the PRD, which says a simulator stands in for
the clearing partner, but the TDD reads as if the live path exists.
Evidence:
[clearbank-adapter.yml](/components/resources/resources/system/clearbank-adapter.yml),
the external adapters
[application.yml](/projects/external-adapters-service/resources/application.yml)
and [values.yaml](/infra/helm/queenswood/values.yaml).

Fix: say in the TDD that only the simulator is reachable, and list
what a live integration needs: the ClearBank URL and credentials,
request signing, webhook signature verification, and registration done
outside the adapter. Name the URL setting for what it points at.

### F8. Medium — the store-layer safety net is not what the TDD says

The TDD says that if a retry carries a different `Idempotency-Key`,
the payment indexes reject the second write. They cannot, because
the index is on the key. A retry with a new key makes a second
payment. The TDD adds that callers "will most often see
`:transaction/already-recorded` rather than
`:payment/already-submitted`". Neither kind is raised anywhere. A
repeated key reads the original payment back and returns it as
ACCEPTED. The API still documents a 409 `AlreadySubmitted` that
nothing produces. Evidence: `or-already-submitted` in the payment
[core.clj](/components/payment/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/payment/core.clj),
the payment
[routes.clj](/bases/api/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/api/payment/routes.clj),
and `rejection-kind->status` in the API
[errors.clj](/bases/api/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/api/errors.clj).

Fix: rewrite the bullet to say that the indexes catch a redelivered
command, and that the answer is the original payment. Remove the 409
from both submit routes, or raise the kind where it is meant to apply.

### F9. Medium — policies apply to inbound payments unevenly

The TDD never mentions policies. The code checks capabilities and
daily limits on every internal and outbound submission and every
settled inbound, but not the same way on every inbound path:

- A settled inbound can be refused, and per F3 is then lost.
- A held inbound runs no check at all. Its release skips the checks,
  with the docstring saying "the payment was already accepted (and
  counted) when it was held". Nothing accepted it: `hold-inbound`
  only saves the record. An inbound the bank's policy would refuse is
  credited in full if ClearBank happens to hold it first.
- An inbound parked in suspense skips the checks, which is consistent
  with parking but is not stated anywhere.

Evidence: `record-inbound-settlement`, `hold-inbound` and
`record-inbound-release` in the payment
[events.clj](/components/payment/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/payment/events.clj),
and [policy-evaluation.md](../policy-evaluation.md).

Fix: decide what an inbound policy may do to money that has already
arrived, and state it in the TDD with a link to the policy TDD. If the
answer is to refuse, apply it equally to the held path and park the
refused amount in suspense. Correct the release docstring.

### F10. Medium — nothing reads an inbound payment

The PRD says a customer reads "the inbound payment record directly".
The API has routes for an internal and an outbound payment by id, and
none for an inbound payment, nor a list of payments of any kind.
`payment-query` can load an inbound payment only by
`scheme-transaction-id`, or a held one by end-to-end id. A `held`,
`returned` or `suspended` inbound is
invisible through the API. A suspended receipt shows only as a balance
on 2500, with no record to reconcile it against, though the TDD says
it is "recorded for later reconciliation". Evidence: the payment
[routes.clj](/bases/api/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/api/payment/routes.clj).

Fix: add a read of an inbound payment by id, scoped by bank per F5, and
a way to list a bank's suspended inbounds for its operator. Describe
the payment read surface in the TDD, or link the document that does.

### F11. Low — the TDD has not caught up with the code

- **Shape.** No Status banner, and the section is still Proposed
  Solution for a built design. There is no `### Tests`. Out of scope
  names fee semantics with no link, the References call idempotency
  "(proposed)" though it is implemented, and the PRD is not linked.
- **Scope.** The brick list omits `payment-query`,
  `clearbank-relay`, `clearbank-webhook` and `payee-check`.
- **The internal flow diagram** saves InternalPayment with
  `status=settled`. It has no status.
- **The inbound table** misses these rows:
  - A settled credit to a BBAN whose account is not opened is parked
    in suspense, and a hold for it stays `held`.
  - A hold for an account that is not opened is ignored.
  - A duplicate settlement of a `suspended` payment is a no-op.
- **The outbound table** misses `failed` + `transaction-settled`
  (F6).
- **The changelog relay** for the outbox runs in the exclusive
  dispatchers service, not the adapter, per ADR-0019.
- **Confirmation of Payee.** Results are now persisted by the
  `payee-check` processor with its own API routes. The limitation
  that says CoP lives only in the adapter is stale.
- **Ordering** is serial because every payment topic has one partition
  and the service one replica. The outbox events carry no ordering
  key, so a second partition would reorder a hold and its settlement.
- **Tone.** "Worth a future reconciliation-sweep design", "Worth
  flagging" and two uses of "today" break the docs rule.

Fix: add the banner as implemented and rename the section Solution.
Add a Tests section listing the scenarios above. Correct the diagram,
the tables, the relay's home, CoP and ordering, and link the PRD and
policy TDD.

### F12. Low — the code describes itself wrongly

- The `settle-inbound` interface docstring says the posting is
  DEBIT 2500 / CREDIT creditor. It is DEBIT 1100.
  `inbound-payment->transaction` names that parameter
  `suspense-account-id`, and a comment in `record-inbound-settlement`
  says suspense is "a workflow for a later wave".
- The `payment` and `payment-query` namespace docstrings name
  `bank-payment-query`, `bank-payment` and `bank-api`, which are now
  `payment-query`, `payment` and `api`.
- The simulator's FPS handler says a held payment is resolved "via the
  /simulate/outbound-held endpoints". No such endpoint exists: it
  always declines.

Fix: correct each, and rename the parameter to the account it
receives.

## Missing evidence

- No test uses an amount with pence through the adapter (F1).
- No test sends two inbound webhooks with the same end-to-end id and
  different transaction ids, or a repeated id across a hold and a
  settlement (F2).
- No test makes an event handler fail in a running consumer and checks
  where the event ends up. The inbound limit is asserted only against
  the domain, where a rejection is a return value (F3).
- No test lets the outbound relay reach its attempt cap, fails a
  publish, redelivers a submit after its commit, or sends the
  misspelled assessment payload (F4).
- No scenario reads a payment with another bank's token (F5).
- No test settles a `failed` outbound, or rejects a `completed` one
  (F6).
- No test runs the adapter against anything but the simulator (F7).
- No test posts two payments with different keys and the same body, and
  no test expects the 409 the API documents (F8).
- No test holds, then releases, an inbound that a policy would refuse
  (F9).
- Outbound held then released is not exercised, as the TDD says. The
  model-equality property test generates no hold, rejection or
  suspense for outbound payments, and no inbound hold. Those paths
  have hand-written scenarios only.
- Inbound held then released or returned is covered by domain
  scenarios that call the payment brick directly. No API scenario
  drives it through the simulator's `outcome` and the adapter's
  mapping.

## Recommended fixes

In order:

1. Convert webhook amounts exactly, with a pence scenario (F1).
2. Deduplicate inbound settlements on the scheme's transaction id, and
   match holds by end-to-end id and BBAN (F2).
3. Scope payment reads by bank, with a cross-bank scenario (F5).
4. Dead-letter and alert on the payment event consumer, back off
   between redeliveries, and park money a policy refuses rather than
   refusing it (F3, F9).
5. Close every path that leaves an outbound pending: a rejection when
   the relay gives up or ClearBank refuses, a republish on redelivery,
   both assessment spellings, and the reconciliation sweep (F4).
6. Guard `settle-outbound` on `pending` or `held` (F6).
7. Add the inbound payment read and a suspended-inbound list (F10).
8. Correct the store-layer idempotency bullet and the 409 the API
   documents (F8).
9. Bring the TDD in line with the code, including the live-integration
   gap, and correct the code's own docstrings (F7, F11, F12).
10. Add the tests listed under Missing evidence.
