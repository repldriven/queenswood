# Webhooks: gap analysis

Subject: [webhooks.md](../webhooks.md) — Webhooks.

## Verdict

**Fail.**

The TDD is a proposal, and its opening line holds: nothing under
Proposed Solution exists. There is no `webhook` component, no
`<domain>-api` or `api-schema` brick, no record type, route, capability,
rejection kind or Standard Webhooks header anywhere in the workspace,
and none of the event names the catalogue would add is registered. That
absence is what the TDD predicts and is not why the verdict fails. It
fails because the design as written cannot deliver three of the PRD's
guarantees, and because the Background misdescribes the base the first
slice extracts from:

- The notification's dedup key is the changelog envelope's event id,
  which the relay does not carry onto the bus. Every publish mints a
  fresh id, so a relay redrive produces a second notification under a
  second identifier, and "safe to receive twice" fails at the source
  (F1).
- The delivery record keeps an attempt count and the last response.
  The PRD requires every attempt, when it was made and what the
  endpoint answered, and the operator's dispute case rests on it (F2).
- "Every read route projects onto the keys its Malli component
  declares" is true of cash accounts alone. Every other domain returns
  the record verbatim, and response coercion neither strips nor rejects
  an undeclared key, so the `->body` each `<domain>-api` is to expose
  exists for one of the eight resources the `oneOf` names (F3).
- The one cash-account event carries a change kind the envelope drops,
  so a new payment address and a move to another product arrive as a
  status change whose before and after are equal, and a two-phase open
  produces two notifications (F4).
- Two PRD catalogue entries have no home, three `oneOf` members have no
  tenant read route to be equal to, and the capitalisation entry names
  a transaction id nothing persists (F5).
- The idempotency key the envelope carries is the request that created
  the record, not the one the change completes (F6). The operator's
  view, the console and test-versus-live are absent without being
  scoped out (F7). The one-replica runner contradicts ADR-0019 and the
  TDD's own tolerance argument (F8). The tests it says it keeps do not
  exist and the rig cannot host the receiver (F9).

The verdict flips to pass when the relay carries the changelog's event
id and the notification is keyed on it, an attempt record exists, the
Background describes the projection and the file layout as they are
and each slice includes writing the projection, the envelope carries
the change kind and the catalogue says which leg of a two-phase
transition is told, the PRD surfaces the TDD does not build are named
as out of scope, and the hosting position is reconciled with ADR-0019.

## What matches

- The envelope carries the eight fields the TDD names, plus a
  `created_at` it omits. The tenant's identity is not on it.
- The cash-accounts, parties and idvs stores and the two adapter
  outboxes write it and are the five stores a runner relays, one topic
  each, all in `exclusive-dispatchers-service`. The bank store writes
  both its events and no runner tails it. The payment, interest,
  transaction, product and migration bricks write no changelog and
  publish no event; the payment brick's only publish is the
  `submit-payment` command.
- No `changelog-relay/event-consumer` kind is registered; the brick
  registers `runner`, `runners` and `envelope-handler`. Every consumer
  is mono's `event-processor/event-processor` kind around a per-brick
  processor record, and at the pinned tag that kind wraps the handler
  in `try-nom` and rethrows an anomaly, so the bus redelivers, bounded
  by `max-redeliveries` with a dead-letter producer. The TDD is right
  and the documents it calls drifted are wrong (F13).
- The ClearBank runner reads pending intents by a status index, calls
  out with no FDB transaction open, records the outcome in its own,
  gives up at a fixed ten attempts, and sleeps a fixed two hundred
  milliseconds with no backoff and no due-time field. It is a named
  daemon thread wrapped in a component kind, not the scheduler brick,
  which is per-bank cron at daily or coarser periodicity.
- Neither inbound receiver verifies anything: the adapters' interceptor
  chains are context injection, the routes carry no security metadata,
  and no adapter YAML holds a secret.
- `clearbank-webhook` exposes exactly a component registry and an
  example registry, both consumed by the adapter and the simulator.
- A record type is registered in the union proto and the record-type
  YAML as the TDD says. The next free union slots are 39 to 41.
- Rejections map as the TDD says: an explicit override, else a name
  ending `not-found` is 404, `already-exists` or `duplicate` is 409,
  anything else 422. The products precedent is a direct interface call
  from the base with capability and count checks in `domain.clj` on
  policies `core.clj` resolves. Paths are flat `/v1/<resource>` with
  the bank taken from the credential.
- The document Reitit emits is `3.1.0`. `CashAccount`, `Party`,
  `OutboundPayment`, `InternalPayment`, `Transaction` and `Bank` are
  registered components.
- Cash-account opening and closing are two-phase, `opening` to
  `opened` through the brick's own event handler. Every topic is a
  single partition, and the four domain writers set an ordering key;
  the two adapter outboxes do not.
- A bank's status is `TEST` or `LIVE`, and the brick writes
  `bank-status-changed` and `bank-tier-changed` envelopes for both
  transitions.
- Both scenario rigs boot the API, both adapters and both simulators as
  in-process Jetty servers on port zero, with handlers patched in and
  the base URL read off the live server.

## Findings

Severity is High where a stated guarantee is broken, Medium where the
design and the code or the PRD disagree in a way a reader would act on,
Low where the document is incomplete.

### F1. High — the dedup key the consumer needs never reaches it

Hearing a change says "The notification's dedup key is the envelope's
event id, under a unique index, so a redelivered event is a no-op". The
`ChangelogEvent` proto calls `event_id` "the consumer's dedup key", and
ADR-0021 repeats it. The relay does not carry it. The envelope handler
destructures `event-name`, `payload`, `correlation-id`, `causation-id`,
`traceparent` and `ordering-key`, drops `event_id`, `dedup_key` and
`created_at`, and builds a fresh bus envelope whose `:id` mono mints as
a new uuidv7 on every publish. The cash-accounts TDD says the same:
"the relay's envelope decoder does not carry the field onto the
published event".

A bus redelivery repeats one publish and so carries one id; the unique
index would hold. A relay redrive does not: a publish the broker
accepted but the client saw fail is thrown and redriven, and a crash
between the publish and the cursor checkpoint replays the entry. Both
republish under a new id, the consumer writes a second notification
with a second `notification-id`, and the tenant receives one change
twice under two identifiers. That is the case the PRD's "safe to
receive twice" exists to prevent. The existing consumers survive this
because their second legs gate on the record's status, which a
notification writer cannot do. `occurred-at` has the same problem: its
source, `created_at`, stops at the relay too. Traced, not executed.
Evidence: `->handler` in the changelog-relay brick's `envelope.clj`,
`envelope` in mono's event `publisher.clj`, the field comments in
[changelog-event.proto](/components/schema/resources/schemas/changelog/changelog-event.proto),
and the change-kind section of
[cash-accounts.md](../cash-accounts.md).

Fix: carry `event_id`, `dedup_key` and `created_at` onto the bus
envelope in the relay, which is generic and which existing consumers
ignore, key the notification's unique index on the changelog event id,
and make that relay change the first step of slice 2. Correct ADR-0021,
whose envelope list also lacks `ordering_key`.

### F2. High — one row per delivery, where the PRD requires every attempt

The PRD's history section says a tenant lists deliveries "each with its
notification, every attempt, when each was made, and what the endpoint
answered", its goals say "Every attempt is recorded with its outcome",
and the operator's view settles a dispute over "what the tenant was
told and when". The Records section gives `WebhookDelivery` an
`attempts` count, when the next attempt is due, and "the last response
status or error". Earlier attempts' times and answers are gone once the
next one runs, and the runner overwrites the one response it keeps. The
re-send rule, "recorded alongside the original attempts", presumes the
history the model does not keep, and the TDD does not say whether a
re-send is a new delivery row. The history filter on kind is a join the
TDD does not describe: kind lives on the notification and the delivery
index is endpoint and time.

Fix: add a `WebhookDeliveryAttempt` record keyed by delivery, carrying
attempted-at, the response status or error and the duration, indexed by
delivery, or a repeated field on the delivery; state that a re-send is
a new delivery of the same notification; denormalise kind and outcome
onto the delivery or index them.

### F3. High — the projection the design moves exists for one domain

Background says "Every read route in the `api` base loads a record
through the domain's `*-query` brick and projects it onto the keys its
Malli component declares, so a stored field the component does not
name cannot reach a response body", and that a domain's public shape
is four files. Only the cash-account queries do this: `->body` selects
`cash-account-keys`, which is derived from the `CashAccount` component,
and a test holds it. The ledger-account and jobs queries hand-rename;
the party, payment, bank, balance, product, migration, policy, tier
and transaction queries return the brick's record verbatim. Response
coercion is in mono's router chain, but the base sets
`:strip-extra-keys false` and every response body is an open `[:ref]`
schema, so nothing strips or rejects an undeclared key. The chart of
accounts gap report found ledger-account `status` reaching every body
this way; a party's stored `idempotency_key` reaches its body the same
way.

The consequence for the design is that `<domain>-api`'s `->body` is
not a move for any domain but the first. Each later extraction must
write the projection, or the notification's `data` inherits whatever
the record leaks and the slice's equality assertion passes because
both sides leak alike. The layout claim is also off: five of the domain
folders carry `links.clj`, five lack `coercion.clj`, and the
transaction folder has components and coercion only. The helpers the
`api-schema` extraction names live in `schema.clj`, `coercion.clj` and
`errors.clj` at the base root, not in `shared/components.clj`, and
every entity id format is defined in its own domain; only `id-schema`
is shared. Evidence: `->body` and `cash-account-keys` in the api base's
cash-account `queries.clj` and `components.clj`, the coercion options
in its `api.clj`, `->api` in its ledger-account `queries.clj`, and
`get-party` in its party `queries.clj`.

Fix: rewrite the Background to say cash accounts project and nothing
else does; add "write the `->body` projection and its declared-keys
test" to every slice's extraction step; name the files that actually
move.

### F4. High — the cash-account envelope cannot tell the PRD's changes apart

The PRD lists an account "opened, closed, frozen, unfrozen, moved to
another product, or given a new payment address" as distinct things
to be told. One event exists, `cash-account-status-changed`, and its
payload's `change_kind` (open, close, suspend, resume, rotate-address,
migrate) is what distinguishes the writes: rotation and migration
leave the status alone, so `status_before` equals `status_after` on
both. The notification body lists `status-before` and `status-after`
and no change kind, under one public kind named `status-changed`. A
tenant receives a status change whose statuses are equal and cannot
say whether the address or the product moved without diffing the
resource.

Opening is two-phase and every `save-account` co-commits an entry,
including both legs, so one opening is two envelopes: to `opening`,
then `opening` to `opened`. The consumer as specified writes a
notification for every catalogued event, so the tenant is told twice,
the first time about a status it has no use for. Closing does the
same. The TDD picks cash accounts for this two-phase shape and does
not say which leg is told. Evidence: the `change_kind` symbols in
[account-status-changed.avsc.json](/components/schema/resources/schemas/cash-accounts/account-status-changed.avsc.json),
`complete-status-transition` in the cash-account brick's `core.clj`, and the
lifecycle and change-kind sections of
[cash-accounts.md](../cash-accounts.md).

Fix: carry `change-kind` in the envelope, or map change kinds to public
kinds so an address rotation and a migration have their own names;
state that the consumer tells the terminal leg of a two-phase
transition and skips the intermediate one, and how it knows which is
which.

### F5. Medium — catalogue entries with no event, no route, or no id

- **Cash account products.** The PRD's catalogue has "a version
  published" and "a bulk move of accounts completed". Neither appears
  in the TDD's catalogue, its slices, or its list of what the
  processors must publish. Neither brick writes a changelog or has an
  events namespace; publish is a synchronous status flip.
- **Resources without a tenant read route.** `data` is "the resource
  exactly as its read route returns it". No `InboundPayment` component
  exists and the payment routes are outbound and internal only. A
  `Transaction` is served only as an element of the list under an
  account, with no single read and no projection. `Bank` is served
  under admin-only routes; a tenant reads its own bank through
  `/v1/me`, which returns a `Me` shape. Three of the eight `oneOf`
  members therefore have no tenant body to equal.
- **The capitalisation id.** The entry is "`Transaction`, the posting
  the run made", published "carrying the posting transaction id". The
  run posts one transaction per account and an aggregate ledger entry
  per currency and product type, and the transaction id is bound
  inside `capitalize-account` and persisted nowhere:
  `InterestAccountRun` has no transaction field.
- **`bank_id` in every payload.** Six of the nine registered event
  payloads carry it. The three scheme-level events do not, so the
  sentence is false as written, and the new payment events the design
  depends on for isolation are not required to carry it.

Evidence: `publish` in the product brick's `domain.clj`, the route
vectors in the api base's payment and cash-account `routes.clj`, `Me`
in its me `components.clj`, `capitalize-account` in the interest
brick's `capitalize.clj`, the run message in
[interest-run.proto](/components/schema/resources/schemas/interest/interest-run.proto),
and the scheme payment schemas under `schemas/schemes/payments/`.

Fix: add the two product entries with their events, or name them as
out of scope; add an `InboundPayment` component and read route, a
single-transaction read and a tenant-facing bank shape before their
slices; emit the capitalisation envelope where the transaction id is
bound or persist it on the account run; require `bank_id` on every new
payload.

### F6. Medium — the idempotency key travels for the wrong request

The envelope carries "the `idempotency-key` where the record carries
one", to meet the PRD's "where the change completes something the
tenant asked for". Per the idempotency TDD, a record's key is the key
of the request that created it: opening, submission, creation. Close,
suspend, resume and rotate stamp nothing beyond a second field,
`last_rotation_idempotency_key`, and the Idv record has no key at all.
So a notification for an account frozen carries the opening request's
key, which is the one thing the PRD says the key is for matching
against. Only the payment entries, where settlement completes the
submission, and openings themselves satisfy the PRD, and the payment
entries have no events. The key is also a new exposure: no API
component publishes it and the cash-account projection removes it, so
the consumer must read envelope fields from the un-projected record,
which the TDD does not say. Evidence: the route inventory in
[idempotency.md](../idempotency.md), the key fields in
[account.proto](/components/schema/resources/schemas/cash-accounts/account.proto),
and the ns docstring of the api base's cash-account `queries.clj`.

Fix: state per kind which key travels, and that a transition carries
none unless its command's key is written into the changelog payload;
say the envelope is built from the record before projection.

### F7. Medium — PRD surfaces neither built nor scoped out

- **The operator's view.** The PRD's functional scope has an operator
  seeing delivery health across tenants, pausing an endpoint, and
  reading any tenant's history. The TDD has no admin route, names no
  security scheme on any route, and its delivery indexes, status with
  due time and endpoint with time, answer no cross-tenant question.
- **The console.** The PRD's self-service goal and the pause behaviour
  both name the management console. The TDD's one mention is a Known
  Limitation saying a pause reaches it; no console surface exists and
  none is in scope.
- **Test and live.** The PRD's goal and section have no counterpart.
  A bank's status is `TEST` or `LIVE` on one record and the flip
  re-audiences its credential, so endpoints follow the bank and
  `bank-status-changed` is the move; the TDD should say so rather than
  leave it to be inferred.
- **The gap on re-enable.** The PRD couples re-enabling with "the
  choice of sending the gap" and the journey says "re-send since the
  first failure". The TDD offers `enable` and a window re-send as two
  uncoupled calls, with the tenant computing the window.
- **The test notification.** The PRD's tenant sends one "and see[s]
  whether it was acknowledged"; the TDD has the route and no answer
  shape.

Fix: add the operator routes and a per-bank health read, or list them
with the console under out of scope; add a sentence on test and live;
give re-enable an optional since parameter; say what the test route
returns.

### F8. Medium — one replica in a group ADR-0019 keeps free of the pin

Delivering says "The runner is hosted at one replica", then that a
second replica's double-send "is tolerable", and Known Limitations
repeat the tolerance. ADR-0019 says exclusive work "lives in one group,
nowhere else" and that every other group "is free of the constraint
precisely because it hosts none". The TDD attributes the placement to
what ADR-0019 "puts there: an intent store and calls to the outside";
the ADR puts external-vendor adapters there, and the intent store is
its reason, not its rule. The precedent is real but unwritten: the
ClearBank and Onfido intent runners already run in that group, every
service is at one replica because the chart's default is one, and only
`exclusive-dispatchers-service` carries a comment saying why. The TDD
never resolves whether the runner is pinned, in which case it belongs
in the exclusive group, or tolerant, in which case the sentence should
go.

The consumer is under-wired. `external-adapters-service` hosts command
consumers and no event consumer today; mono's kind takes one
`event-channel`, so "subscribed, by configuration, to every relayed
event topic" is one instance per topic, each needing a consumer group
that ADR-0019 says moves verbatim thereafter, and none is named. The
component also reads every catalogued domain's records through its
`*-query` brick, which ADR-0021's "acts only on its own records" rule
does not contemplate; the TDD calls it the honest cost and does not
amend the rule. Evidence: the replica values and comments in
[values.yaml](/infra/helm/queenswood/values.yaml), the consumers block
of the external-adapters `application.yml`, and the rules sections of
[ADR-0019](../../adr/0019-processor-packaging.md) and
[ADR-0021](../../adr/0021-changelog-relay.md).

Fix: choose pinned or tolerant and write the consequence; record the
intent-runner exception in ADR-0019 either way; list the consumer
instances and their groups; add the cross-domain read as a stated
exception to ADR-0021's sixth rule.

### F9. Medium — tests the TDD keeps do not exist, and the rig has no receiver

Tests says the `api` base "keeps a test that the exported document
resolves every `$ref`". The base's test folder has no OpenAPI test;
the pages workflow exports the document and validates nothing, so
ADR-0014's "validated against the OpenAPI 3.x schema in CI" is unmet
and slice 1's "confirm the exported OpenAPI document is unchanged" has
no harness. `oneOf` with a `discriminator` appears nowhere in the base:
every `oneOf` in the exported document is a nullable projection and
there are no discriminators, so the notification component is the
first use and the Malli-to-discriminator projection is unproven.

The end-to-end scenario wants "a receiver the test rig hosts" and an
assertion that `data` "equals the read route's response", and calls
that equality "the design's contract". `test-api-scenarios` boots the
simulators but hosts no receiver and has no verb for one; its
assertions are matcher-combinators shape markers, not equality, and
the equality property belongs to the domain-layer brick. Its
quiescence waits on changelog cursor catch-up, and the webhook path
adds a bus hop, a consumer commit, a runner poll and an HTTP call after
that. The signing test names "the specification's test vectors", and
none are in the repository. Evidence: the api base's `test/` listing,
the export step in
[pages.yml](/.github/workflows/pages.yml), the runner and verbs of the
`test-api-scenarios` brick, and the assertion and quiescence sections
of [scenario-testing.md](../scenario-testing.md).

Fix: add the OpenAPI snapshot and validation test before slice 1 step
1; prove the discriminator projection in a spike; add a receiver verb,
an equality assertion and a delivery-quiescence wait to the API
scenario runner as part of slice 2.

### F10. Medium — delivery to a tenant URL needs guards the design omits

The runner is the intent runner generalised, and the intent runner
sends with no timeout: the http-client brick passes `:timeout`,
`:follow-redirects` and `:insecure?` through to http-kit and neither
existing runner sets any. A tenant endpoint that never answers blocks
the single drain thread for every tenant. A redirect defeats the
address rule: registration refuses a private address, and a public
address that answers 302 to one is followed at delivery. No response
size is bounded. Known Limitations note the re-resolution gap and not
these. Evidence: the ns docstring of mono's http-client `interface.clj`,
`post-fps` in the clearbank-relay brick's `outbound.clj`, and `post` in
the onfido-relay brick's `outbound.clj`.

Fix: set a timeout, refuse redirects, resolve and re-check the host at
send time, cap the body read, and bound concurrency per endpoint so one
slow tenant cannot starve the rest.

### F11. Medium — the write routes owe the idempotency pair

The idempotency TDD requires every `/v1` write route to declare the
interceptor pair or be named in `exempt-writes`, and a router test
holds both directions. The TDD adds create, update, enable, disable,
remove, rotate, test-send and two re-sends and says nothing about it;
the first route to land fails the coverage test. ADR-0018's products
precedent is retry-safe because a retried create reads back off the
`[bank_id, idempotency_key]` index; `WebhookEndpoint` carries no key
and no such index, so a retried registration mints two endpoints, two
secrets and two counts against the limit. "None of the four" is also
too strong: the endpoint store's status writes have reaction, since
the component's own consumer turns them into notifications; that is
defensible and unargued. Evidence: the coverage rules in
[idempotency.md](../idempotency.md), `idempotency_coverage_test.clj` in
the api base's tests, and the four properties in
[ADR-0018](../../adr/0018-command-writes-are-earned.md).

Fix: add `idempotency_key` and its unique index to `WebhookEndpoint`,
list the pair or the exemption per route, and say why the endpoint's
reaction does not make registration a command.

### F12. Low — Background details a builder would copy wrongly

- "Both YAMLs" is one: `avro-schemas.yml`. The recipe's `bank/avro.yml`
  and `avro-test.yml` do not exist, and the recipe's requirement that
  the channel, topic and consumer be declared "including the monolith
  and the test rigs" is omitted.
- An intent's status is `pending`, `sent` or `failed`, a string with a
  comment rather than an enum; "attempted" is not a status, since
  `mark-attempt` bumps the count and leaves the row pending.
- "The receiver has a dedup key" holds for ClearBank, whose end-to-end
  id travels in the body, and not for the Onfido runner, whose two
  calls carry none.
- Registering a record type has a third site: the `pb->`, `->pb` and
  `->java` trio per type in the schema brick's `interface.clj`.
- The products precedent backs its count limit with an FDB `count`
  index and checks the count plus one at `time-window-instant` in
  `domain.clj`; Records says "indexed by bank" and names no count
  index.
- The transport is Kafka, per the topic list and the consumer kinds,
  while the design rule still names Pulsar as production.
- The `:<entity>/invalid-status` payload the recipe mandates,
  `:message`, the id key, `:status` and `:allowed`, is not stated.

Fix: correct each sentence; name the count index and the conversion
trio in Records.

### F13. Low — the cited documents disagree with the TDD, and the TDD is right

- ADR-0021 rule 3, the lifecycle recipe's ninth point, the
  processor-bricks TDD, the design rule and the relay plan all say
  mono's `event-processor` "acks on anomaly" and prescribe
  `changelog-relay/event-consumer`. At the pinned tag the processor
  rethrows, and the kind does not exist. The TDD calls this "one drift
  affects naming here" and leaves the four documents standing.
- ADR-0021's envelope list omits `ordering_key`, and its Future section
  proposes deriving a partition key from `causation_id`, which the
  proto's own comment rejects.
- The payments and parties TDDs say their receivers verify signatures.
  Neither does. The TDD's "Neither inbound receiver verifies a
  signature" is the accurate sentence.
- The service-apis TDD names two security schemes; the document
  declares one, `bearerAuth`, with roles.

Fix: correct ADR-0021, the recipe, the processor-bricks TDD and the
design rule; fix the receiver claims in the two sibling TDDs; have the
TDD say the drift is in those documents.

## Missing evidence

- Nothing exercises a relay redrive. No test crashes a runner between
  publish and checkpoint or fails a publish the broker accepted, so the
  new-id-per-redrive path F1 turns on has never been observed.
- No test drives any read body but cash accounts against its
  component's declared keys, so the leak F3 describes is proven for
  ledger accounts by the earlier gap report and inferred elsewhere.
- No test opens an account and counts the envelopes, so the
  two-notifications-per-open case in F4 is traced from the store's
  co-commit rule, not shown.
- No OpenAPI validation or `$ref` test exists, and no `oneOf` with a
  `discriminator` has ever been projected from this base.
- The API scenario runner has no receiver verb, no equality assertion
  and no wait past cursor catch-up.
- Neither existing runner sets a timeout, and no test sends to a slow
  or redirecting endpoint.
- The bank's events have no relay runner, so `bank-status-changed` and
  `bank-tier-changed` have never been consumed by anything.
- No Standard Webhooks vectors are vendored, and whether the
  specification publishes a vector file rather than per-library tests
  is unverified.

## Recommended fixes

In order:

1. Carry `event_id`, `dedup_key` and `created_at` across the relay, key
   the notification on the event id, and correct ADR-0021 (F1, F13).
2. Add a `WebhookDeliveryAttempt` record, define a re-send as a new
   delivery, and index kind and outcome on the delivery (F2).
3. Rewrite the Background on projection and layout, and add "write the
   projection and its declared-keys test" to every slice (F3).
4. Carry `change-kind` in the envelope or split the kinds, and say which
   leg of a two-phase transition is told (F4).
5. Add the product entries or scope them out; add an `InboundPayment`
   component and read route, a single-transaction read and a tenant
   bank shape ahead of their slices; emit or persist the capitalisation
   transaction id; require `bank_id` on every new payload (F5).
6. State per kind which idempotency key travels, and that envelope
   fields come from the un-projected record; add a key and index to
   `WebhookEndpoint` and list the interceptor pair per route (F6, F11).
7. Add or scope out the operator routes, the console and test versus
   live; couple re-enable with a since parameter; define the test
   route's answer (F7).
8. Decide pinned or tolerant for the runner and record the exception in
   ADR-0019; name the consumer instances and groups; add the
   cross-domain read to ADR-0021 (F8).
9. Add the OpenAPI test before slice 1, spike the discriminator, and
   give the scenario runner a receiver, an equality assertion and a
   delivery wait (F9).
10. Add the timeout, redirect, re-resolution, size and concurrency
    guards to the runner (F10).
11. Correct the Background details and the four stale documents (F12,
    F13).
