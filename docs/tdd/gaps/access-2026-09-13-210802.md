# Access: gap analysis

Subject: [access.md](../access.md) — Access.

## Verdict

**Fail.**

Slice 1 is built close to the design. The records, the declaration at
version 52, the `membership` brick's rules and transactions, the
`Bank-Id` header, the four organisation levels, the sweep of every
route, the router checks and every scenario the Tests section names
are all present, and several go further than the TDD asks. The
console, slice 2, is not started, as the TDD plans.

The verdict fails on what slice 1 left out or got wrong:

- The deployed realm emits no `email_verified` claim, and the code
  requires it. In a deployed installation a signed-in person sees no
  invitation of their own, and accepting without the link always
  answers 404. Only the test realm gained the claim (F1).
- The operator's overview is not built. `GET /v1/banks` carries no
  owners, so nothing lists an organisation that has none (F2).
- The API answers "who invited" and "who acted" with an id nobody
  outside the bank's current members can turn into a name. So slice 2
  cannot add "no route", as the TDD says it will (F3).
- The plaintext invitation token is cached for a day in the
  idempotency store, beside a design that says only its hash is kept
  (F4).
- The authentication, service APIs and onboarding TDDs still describe
  the three-word gate and the first-membership bank (F5).

The verdict flips to pass when the deployed realm emits the claim, the
bank list carries owners, the API names the people it refers to, the
cached token is addressed, and the sibling TDDs describe the levels.

## What was examined

- The subject TDD, the access PRD, and the TDDs it cites:
  authentication, banks, idempotency, onboarding and service APIs.
- The commit that landed slice 1 (#643), file by file.
- The `api` base: `auth.clj`, the router assembly and its enforcement
  checks, `errors.clj`, every `routes.clj`, the access and `me`
  handlers and components, the bank create path, onboarding, and the
  shared parameters and idempotency exemptions.
- The `membership` brick: `domain.clj`, `core.clj`, `store.clj` and
  `interface.clj`. The `bank` brick's `core.clj`, `domain.clj`,
  `commands.clj` and `interface.clj`.
- The `Membership`, `Invitation`, `AccessEvent` and `Actor` protos, the
  `create-bank` and `bank` Avro schemas, and
  [fdb-record-types.yml](/components/resources/resources/system/fdb-record-types.yml).
- The deployed and test realm imports, and the Helm copy of the
  deployed realm.
- The idempotency interceptor and store, and the id generator in the
  pinned `mono`.
- Every access scenario under `test-api-scenarios`, and the
  `membership`, `bank` and `api` tests the slice added.
- The console's `App.svelte` and request wrapper.

Nothing was executed. Every finding below was traced in source, and the
ones that predict runtime behaviour say so.

## What matches

- **The header.** `user-auth` resolves the named bank to an active
  membership, or with no header the only one. A service principal
  naming another bank is refused, and an operator takes the header's
  bank with every level. Each refusal carries the detail the TDD
  gives, and a `user` route ignores a stale header.
- **The levels.** `role->levels` gives each role its level and every
  one below it, and a service principal carries `org:viewer` and
  `org:developer`. `authorize` keeps its intersection rule.
- **The sweep.** No route names bare `org`. The router refuses a bare
  `org` gate, a scheme with no roles, and a method level stacked on a
  route level. That last check is not in the TDD. A test walks the
  real route table and asserts every organisation read is
  `org:viewer`, every write `org:developer` and every people write
  `org:admin`. The simulator and companies exceptions keep their
  shape, and every organisation operation lists the header once.
- **The records.** `Membership` gains optional status, `ended_at`,
  `ended_by` and `invitation_id`, and the three roles are uncommented
  at their reserved numbers. `Invitation` and `AccessEvent` carry the
  fields, keys and indexes the TDD lists. The declaration bumps once to
  52, the new stores carry it as `since`, and the retired unique index
  is a former entry.
- **The rules.** Who may invite, change a role, remove and leave match
  the PRD's roles, an operator acts as an owner, and the ownerless
  guard reads active memberships inside the writing transaction. The
  one-pending, already-member and one-active-membership rules and the
  source-state guards are there, with `now` passed in. Expiry is read,
  never written.
- **The token.** It is 32 bytes from `SecureRandom`, base64url without
  padding, stored as its SHA-256, travels in `Invitation-Token`, and is
  answered by create and resend only.
- **The writes.** Every access write is synchronous, one transaction
  each, with its event. `new-bank` drops the sole-membership check and
  writes the creation event and the operator's owner invitation in the
  bank's transaction.
- **The routes.** Every route the TDD lists exists at its gate. Create
  and resend carry the idempotency pair, the rest are exempt with the
  guards the TDD names, and the fingerprint includes the resolved bank.
  The rejection kinds map to the statuses the TDD gives.
- **The tests.** Every brick test and every scenario the Tests section
  names exists. Concurrent owner demotions and concurrent accepts by
  one person are covered as well.

## Findings

Severity is High where a stated guarantee is broken, Medium where the
design and the code disagree in a way a reader would act on, Low where
a document is incomplete.

### F1. High — a deployed realm never proves an email

Traced, not executed. A recipient proves themselves by the token or by
a verified email: `check-recipient` and `list-my-invitations` both
require the token's `email_verified` claim to be `true`. The slice added
an `email-verified` protocol mapper to the `queenswood-console` client
in the test realm only. The deployed realm, and its Helm copy, map
`email` and no verification, and the realm's declared client scopes
include no `email` scope. The prediction is that a deployed token
carries no `email_verified`, whatever `trustEmail` says, and so in a
deployed installation:

- `GET /v1/me/invitations` always answers an empty list.
- Accepting, declining or reading an invitation without the link
  always answers 404 `invitation/not-found`.

The PRD's "a signed-in person also sees any pending invitation
addressed to the email they signed in with" fails, and so does the
invitations-first landing that slice 2 builds on it. The TDD's own
account of the proof says "the signed-in person's email matches the
invited address" and never mentions verification. Evidence:
[keycloak-realm.json](/components/resources/resources/keycloak-realm.json)
against
[queenswood-realm.json](/components/test-resources/test-resources/queenswood-realm.json),
and `proof` in the access
[handlers.clj](/bases/api/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/api/access/handlers.clj).

Fix: add the same mapper to the deployed realm and the Helm copy, and
confirm a token from a running realm carries the claim. State in the
TDD that the email proof requires a verified address, and which realm
setting provides it.

### F2. High — nothing lists an organisation with no owner

The TDD says the operator's overview is the admin bank list, with
`GET /v1/banks` carrying each bank's active owners, "and an
organisation with none is one whose list is empty". The handler
returns `bank-query/get-banks` unchanged, and the `Bank` component has
no owners. An operator who created a bank with an owner email cannot
see which handovers are still pending, nor find an organisation whose
last owner has gone, except by reading each bank's members in turn.
The PRD's "the operator's view lists every organisation in that state"
has no API behind it. Evidence:
[queries.clj](/bases/api/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/api/bank/queries.clj)
and `Bank` in the bank `components.clj`.

Fix: enrich the list on read with each bank's active owners, add
`owners` to `Bank` with an example, and add a scenario where a bank
created with an owner email lists none until its invitation is
accepted.

### F3. Medium — the API names people by an id nobody can resolve

The PRD has a person see who sent an invitation, and every member see
who made each change, including a removed member and an operator. The
API answers each with an `Actor` of kind and principal id, which is a
user id, or an operator's user id or client id:

- A recipient is not a member, so `/v1/members` is refused, and
  `RecipientInvitation.invited-by` cannot be named.
- `/v1/members` lists active members only, so an ended member's
  `subject-user-id` in the history, and an `invited-by` naming someone
  who has since left, cannot be named.
- An operator-realm user is never a member, so an operator's act is
  shown as an opaque id.

The TDD says slice 2 "adds no route", which these reads contradict.
Evidence: `->actor` and `->recipient-invitation` in the access
[handlers.clj](/bases/api/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/api/access/handlers.clj),
and `Actor` in the access `components.clj`.

Fix: carry a display name on `Actor` in each response, resolved on
read from the `user` brick, with the operator shown as the platform
where there is no user. Say in the TDD what an operator's name is to a
customer.

### F4. Medium — the plaintext token is cached for a day

The TDD says the store keeps the token's SHA-256 and the plaintext is
returned twice. Create and resend carry the idempotency pair, and
`cache-response` writes the whole response body, token included, into
the `idempotency` store for 24 hours. The `invitation-create-replay`
scenario asserts that a replay returns the same token. Anyone who can
read that store holds a live link to every invitation made or resent
that day. The create-bank response caches the client secret the same
way, which is the authentication TDD's concern. Evidence:
`cache-response` in the idempotency
[interceptors.clj](/components/idempotency/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/idempotency/interceptors.clj),
and [idempotency.md](../idempotency.md) on the 24-hour entry.

Fix: decide whether a replay should return the token. If it should,
say in the TDD that the cache holds the plaintext for the window, and
why that is acceptable. If not, cache the response without it and
answer a replay with the invitation and no token.

### F5. Medium — the sibling TDDs still describe the three-word gate

The slice updated [banks.md](../banks.md) and
[idempotency.md](../idempotency.md). It did not update the documents
the access TDD extends:

- [authentication.md](../authentication.md) still gives the principal
  `:bank-id` from the first membership, `:org` from any membership, the
  vocabulary `:user`, `:org` and `:admin`, the simulator as
  `["org" "admin"]`, and a list of routes gated `["org"]`.
- [service-apis.md](../service-apis.md) gives `["org"]` as the example
  gate twice.
- [onboarding.md](../onboarding.md) still describes the unique
  (user, organisation) index and the `:org` grant.

A reader writing a new route from any of them writes a gate the router
now refuses at start-up.

Fix: rewrite the authentication TDD's principal, roles and gate rule
around the header and the four levels, and move its route list to the
rule the access TDD states. Correct the example in service APIs, and
mark the onboarding TDD's record and principal sections as superseded
by access.

### F6. Medium — a repeated role change records a change that did not happen

The exemption list says change-role is an absolute set, "so a second
application converges". The membership converges, but
`domain/change-role` does not refuse a role equal to the current one,
and `core/change-role` writes a `role-changed` event on every run. A
retried request, or a console that re-submits the same role, adds an
event whose role before and after are equal. The PRD's history is
meant to show "each role change", and an auditor reads each row as one.
Evidence: `change-role` in the membership
[domain.clj](/components/membership/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/membership/domain.clj)
and [core.clj](/components/membership/src/com/repldriven/queenswood/membership/core.clj).

Fix: answer an unchanged role with the membership and write no event,
or refuse it with `:membership/invalid-status`. Test that a replay
leaves the history unchanged.

### F7. Low — the TDD has not caught up with its own slice

- **The banner** still says proposal for everything under Proposed
  Solution. Slice 1 is built.
- **Background** describes states the slice removed: the bank from the
  first membership, one organisation per person and its 409, the
  commented roles, and the create call that names no person.
- **The command** is said to carry the owner email. It carries the
  email and the token hash, with the actor, so the plaintext never
  reaches the bus.
- **`GET /v1/invitations`** is said to list pending and expired
  invitations. It also lists accepted ones, and leaves out declined and
  withdrawn, which the inviter sees only in the history.
- **The reason rule** names no kind. An operator's invitation without
  one is `:invitation/reason-required`, 422.
- **Withdraw and resend** also refuse an actor who may not grant the
  invitation's role, so an admin cannot mint a fresh link to an owner
  invitation. The TDD lists neither guard.
- **The router** also refuses a level stacked on a route's level.
- **The creation actor** falls back to an operator with principal id
  `unknown` for a command sent before the field existed.

Fix: move the banner to say slice 1 is built and slice 2 is the build
list, rewrite Background as the state before the slice, and add each
point above to its section.

### F8. Low — a refusal's type has two spellings

The TDD and the PRD rely on the problem's `type` to say whether a role
or a policy refused. The edge writes `auth/forbidden`. A domain refusal
is rendered from its anomaly kind with a leading colon:
`:membership/role-not-granted`, `:policy/denied`. The scenarios assert
both spellings. A client telling the two apart has to match on both.
The spelling is API-wide, and the authentication report and
`auth.clj`'s docstring both note it.

Fix: settle one spelling in [service-apis.md](../service-apis.md), and
until then list the actual strings in the access TDD's "Which of the
two refused".

### F9. Low — the console cannot serve a person with two organisations

Slice 1 lets a person hold several memberships: onboarding no longer
answers 409, and an accepted invitation adds one. The console still
reads the first membership for its kicker and sends no `Bank-Id`, so
every organisation call from a person with two answers 403 "Name the
bank in the Bank-Id header". Slice 2 closes this, but until it ships
the API behaviour already reaches the deployed console. Evidence:
[App.svelte](/bases/console/src/App.svelte) and
[api.mjs](/bases/console/src/lib/api.mjs).

Fix: list it under Known Limitations until slice 2 ships, or send the
first membership's bank as the header meanwhile.

## Missing evidence

- No test runs against the deployed realm's client configuration, and
  the realm files differ in the one claim the email proof reads. No
  token from a deployed realm was decoded to confirm the claim is
  absent (F1).
- Every operator act in the scenarios uses the `queenswood-admin`
  service token. No scenario signs in an operator-realm user and acts
  on a bank with the header, though the TDD names that principal.
- No scenario declines, withdraws or resends an invitation
  successfully, or resends an expired one. Only the refusals reach
  HTTP. The `membership` brick tests cover the successful paths.
- An admin refused a withdraw or resend of an owner invitation is
  covered in the brick only.
- A person creating a second bank through onboarding is covered by a
  handler test with the company lookup redefined. No scenario covers
  it.
- A service credential naming another bank in the header is covered by
  the `auth` unit test only.
- No test asserts that an unchanged role change leaves the history
  unchanged (F6), or that a cached replay omits or includes the token
  by decision (F4).
- The bank list's owners (F2) and a name for each actor (F3) have no
  test, because neither exists.

## Recommended fixes

In order:

1. Add the `email-verified` mapper to the deployed realm and its Helm
   copy, reconcile the running realms, and state the verified-email
   rule in the TDD (F1).
2. Enrich `GET /v1/banks` with active owners, with a scenario (F2).
3. Put a display name on every `Actor` the access routes answer, and
   correct "adds no route" in the slice plan (F3).
4. Decide the token's place in the idempotency cache, then change the
   cache or document it (F4).
5. Stop an unchanged role change from writing an event (F6).
6. Rewrite the authentication TDD's principal and gate sections, and
   correct service APIs and onboarding (F5).
7. Bring the access TDD's banner, Background, invitation list, guards
   and refusal types in line with the code, and record the console gap
   under Known Limitations (F7, F8, F9).
8. Add the scenarios listed under Missing evidence.
