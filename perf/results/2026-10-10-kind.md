# Performance on kind, 10 October 2026

Every scenario under `perf/` at every profile its script defines, run with
`just perf-run` against a fresh `kind-up` on a laptop, from 20:24 to 23:13
UTC. Each run's summary, its `run` and `headline` as k6's `handleSummary`
wrote them, is in [2026-10-10-kind](2026-10-10-kind/). The design these
runs measure, and why kind figures are recorded rather than published, is
[performance-testing](../../docs/tdd/performance-testing.md). The same
runs the day before, on the build without the audit log, are
[2026-10-09-kind](2026-10-09-kind.md).

## The machine

- **Laptop.** MacBook Pro, model `Mac17,9`: Apple M5 Pro, 18 cores (6
  super, 12 performance), 64 GB, macOS 27.0.1.
- **VM.** Colima 0.10.3, `aarch64`, Docker runtime, 8 CPUs, 32 GiB and a
  100 GiB disk, as `just docker-start` sizes it.
- **Cluster.** kind v0.32.0, one node, Kubernetes v1.36.1, inside the VM.
- **Load.** k6 v2.2.0 as a Job in the same cluster, sharing the VM's 8
  CPUs with everything it measures.

## What ran

Smokes at 1 a second for a minute; each scenario's challenger, with
`challenger 50 10m` for internal, outbound, inbound and mixed in place of
their hour-long default, a minute apart; then every knee, three minutes
apart so one run's queues drain before the next starts. A knee runs every
step to the end, past the point the platform keeps up.

## Results

No request the platform accepted was lost, and no run left a payment,
customer or account unanswered. Where money moved, the books held to the
penny: what was injected, paid in and paid out agreed with cash at the
correspondent (1100), the deposit and own-funds controls and the
provider's balances, and the trial balance tied. Every run exited 0; the
accounts and reads knees reported their thresholds unmet, as they did the
day before, past the point they shed load.

## Findings

- **Auditing each act costs nothing measurable below the knee.** Every
  challenger is within a millisecond of the day before at p50 and p99:
  internal 13.4–15.4 ms against 15, outbound 14.2–14.9 against 14–15,
  accounts 12.9 against 12.7. A payment submitted through the API now
  appends its audit entry and its two index keys in the transaction that
  saves it, and its reply carries the payment's bytes, and neither shows.
- **The ceilings are where they were.** 313 internal payments a second at
  320 asked, 307 outbound, 313 inbound, about 130 to 143 account openings
  and 2,506 reads, each within a request a second of the day before.
- **The top steps' tails are wider, by one run.** At 320 asked the outbound
  knee's p99 was 517 ms against 280, the internal's 274 against 223 and
  the mixed knee's 305 against 291, with two 503s answered and retried.
  At 160 asked the internal p99 was lower, 67 against 77. One run each is
  not enough to tell this from a laptop's noise.
- **Settlement with the provider is still the outbound ceiling,** and the
  provider mirror of internal payments still queues on the bank-activity
  topic behind one bank's shard, both to the same depth as the day
  before.
- **Onboarding's verification session slowed at 80 a second:** ready at a
  p99 of 1.35 s against 811 ms, onboarded at a p99 of 1.92 s against
  1.35 s, every queue still under 50.

## Known limitations

- **One run of each.** The comparison with the day before is one run
  against one run, on a laptop whose other work is not controlled.
- **k6 shares the VM.** The load generator takes CPU from the services it
  measures, so every ceiling here is a floor for the same installation on
  dedicated nodes.
- **One bank per run.** Every bank-keyed queue — account openings, the
  bank-activity shard — is measured at one bank's share.
- **Simulated providers.** The Modulr and Zyphe simulators stand in for
  the providers, with no network between them and the adapters.
- **The build is as reported.** The images carry no revision label, so
  the commit is the `main` the images were rebuilt from after #852 merged,
  not one read from the images.
