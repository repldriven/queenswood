# Performance on Google Cloud, 11 October 2026

Every scenario under `perf/` at every profile its script defines, run with
`just perf-run-instance` against release v0.0.9 on a test instance on
Google Kubernetes Engine, from 19:21 to 22:17 UTC. Each run's summary, its
`run` and `headline` as k6's `handleSummary` wrote them, is in
[2026-10-11-gke](2026-10-11-gke/). The design these runs measure is
[performance-testing](../../docs/tdd/performance-testing.md), the procedure
[instance-perf-run](../../docs/recipes/infra/instance-perf-run.md), and the
same suite on a laptop two days earlier
[2026-10-09-kind](2026-10-09-kind.md).

## The machines

- **Cluster.** One GKE cluster in one zone of `europe-west2`, Kubernetes
  v1.35.8.
- **Service nodes.** Three `c3-standard-8`: 8 vCPUs, which are 4 cores of
  two threads each, and 32 GB, with a 50 GB balanced persistent disk to
  boot from.
- **Load node.** One `n2-standard-2`, tainted so nothing but the k6 Job
  runs on it.
- **Volumes.** A 24Gi SSD persistent disk for each FoundationDB storage
  and log process, and a 20Gi balanced persistent disk for each Kafka
  broker.
- **Load.** k6 v2.2.0 as a Job on the load node, reaching the API and the
  simulators by their Services inside the cluster.

## The installation

- **Build.** Release v0.0.9, its images and chart as published.
- **Services.** Replicas and container limits, the chart's defaults:

| Service | Replicas | CPU | Memory |
|---|---|---|---|
| `api-service` | 1 | 1 | 1500Mi |
| `financial-processors-service` | 4 | 1500m | 2Gi |
| `operational-processors-service` | 4 | 1 | 1536Mi |
| `external-adapters-service` | 1 | 1500m | 2Gi |
| `external-simulators-service` | 1 | 1 | 1536Mi |
| `exclusive-dispatchers-service` | 1 | 1 | 1536Mi |
| Kafka 4.3.1 broker | 3 | — | 1Gi |

- **FoundationDB.** 7.3.79 at `double` redundancy, a process per role: 3
  storage, 3 log, 6 stateless and the cluster controller, with three
  coordinators. Each node holds one storage and one log process, so the
  database keeps its data through the loss of a node.
- **Kafka.** Three brokers, one to a node. Every topic is kept on all
  three, and a send waits on two.
- **Topics.** `topic-payments-command`, `topic-schemes-payments-event`,
  `topic-parties-command`, `topic-idvs-command` and `topic-idv-event` at 4
  partitions; every other topic at 1.
- **Providers.** One bank per run, created on the `perf` tier with the
  Modulr simulator for payments and the Zyphe simulator for identity
  verification.
- **Beside it.** SigNoz and its ClickHouse, Keycloak and the operators
  run on the service nodes. Keycloak's database is Cloud SQL.

## What ran

Smokes at 1 a second for a minute; each scenario's challenger, with
`challenger 50 10m` for internal, outbound, inbound and mixed in place of
their hour-long default, a minute apart; then every knee, three minutes
apart so one run's queues drain before the next starts. A knee runs every
step to the end, past the point the platform keeps up. The order and the
pauses are those of the kind runs.

## Results

No payment the platform accepted was lost. Where money moved, the books
balanced to the penny: what was injected, paid in and paid out agreed with
cash at the correspondent (1100), the deposit and own-funds controls and
the provider's balances, and the trial balance tied. No pod restarted
under load.

## Findings

- **Order is preserved end to end, and these are the rates with it.** One
  account's payments are consumed one at a time in the order the API
  accepted them, keyed by the debtor account. Each record's events are
  published in commit order under the record's key. A bank's calls to its
  provider are made in the order its changes committed, first in, first
  out, and a payment's events from the provider are applied in the order
  they arrive. Outbound settlement, the provider mirror and account
  opening each stop at one of those queues: a consumer that may not take
  the next message until it has finished the last. Without the guarantee
  each would spread across consumers and every figure would be higher.
- **Every flow passes its challenger.** At 50 payments or accounts a
  second, 10 customers or 200 reads, each ran ten minutes with no request
  failed, a median near 25 ms and a 99th percentile near 50 ms, about 1.7
  times the laptop's figures with one copy of everything.
- **Internal payments reach 313 a second with none refused.** From 160 a
  second up the instance is level with the kind run; below that a payment
  takes about 10 ms longer, the cost of writes that wait on a second copy.
- **Settlement with the provider is the outbound ceiling,** as on kind.
  The API takes 160 outbound payments a second cleanly; above that the
  Modulr adapter's command consumer falls behind, and an accepted payment
  waits a minute or two to settle, and settles.
- **The provider mirror of internal payments queues on the bank-activity
  topic** at the internal and mixed knees' top steps, behind one bank's
  shard, as on kind.
- **One bank opens about 77 accounts a second.** Its openings are taken
  in turn, each took 12.9 ms in the processor at the 80 step, and that
  one consumer was busy all of the time. On kind the ceiling was 120 to
  140.
- **The API's one CPU is the ceiling for reads and for onboarding.**
  Reads are clean to 320 a second; by the 640 step the API's JVM was
  using all of its CPU limit. At 80 customers a second its FoundationDB
  transactions took 70 ms where they took 5 ms at 40. On kind one CPU was
  a faster core, and reads were clean to 1,280.
- **The API's limit turned load away rather than failing.** Past each
  ceiling it answered 503 at once, k6 retried, and the instance stayed up.

## Known limitations

- **One run of each.** No run was repeated, so no figure here has a
  spread.
- **The data is kept through the loss of a node, and the service is
  not.** `api-service`, `external-adapters-service`,
  `exclusive-dispatchers-service` and Keycloak each ran one replica, and
  all three nodes were in one zone.
- **Telemetry shares the service nodes.** SigNoz's ClickHouse and
  collector take CPU from what they measure.
- **One bank per run.** Every bank-keyed queue — account openings, the
  bank-activity shard — is measured at one bank's share.
- **Simulated providers.** The Modulr and Zyphe simulators stand in for
  the providers, with no network between them and the adapters.
- **k6 follows a payment or an account for two minutes.** Past a knee's
  ceiling some settle or open later than that, and are counted as not
  seen rather than followed to the end.
- **CPU was sampled, not recorded.** Node figures are single readings of
  `kubectl top` at the end of a run.
- **Disk is the suite's total.** It was read once at the end and once
  after the challengers, so what one payment, account or customer takes
  is not separated from the rest.
