# Performance on kind, 9 October 2026

Every scenario under `perf/` at every profile its script defines, run with
`just perf-run` against a fresh `kind-up` on a laptop, from 19:04 to 22:47
UTC. Each run's summary, its `run` and `headline` as k6's `handleSummary`
wrote them, is in [2026-10-09-kind](2026-10-09-kind/). The design these
runs measure, and why kind figures are recorded rather than published, is
[performance-testing](../../docs/tdd/performance-testing.md).

## The machine

- **Laptop.** MacBook Pro, model `Mac17,9`: Apple M5 Pro, 18 cores (6
  super, 12 performance), 64 GB, macOS 27.0.1.
- **VM.** Colima 0.10.3, `aarch64`, Docker runtime, 8 CPUs, 32 GiB and a
  100 GiB disk, as `just docker-start` sizes it.
- **Cluster.** kind v0.32.0, one node, Kubernetes v1.36.1, inside the VM.
- **Load.** k6 v2.2.0 as a Job in the same cluster, sharing the VM's 8
  CPUs with everything it measures.

## What ran

Smokes at 1 a second for a minute; each scenario's challenger, with
`challenger 50 10m` for internal, outbound, inbound and mixed in place of
their hour-long default; then every knee, three minutes apart so one run's
queues drain before the next starts. A knee runs every step to the end,
past the point the platform keeps up.

## Results

No request the platform accepted was lost, and no run left a payment,
customer or account unanswered. Where money moved, the books held to the
penny: what was injected, paid in and paid out agreed with cash at the
correspondent (1100), the deposit and own-funds controls and the
provider's balances, and the trial balance tied.

## Findings

- **The API accepts more than any earlier run.** 313 internal payments a
  second at 320 asked, and 308 outbound, against about 257 and 242 on kind
  before this branch.
- **Settlement with the provider is the outbound ceiling.** Above 160 a
  second the Modulr adapter's command consumer falls behind: an accepted
  payment waits about a minute to settle, and settles.
- **The provider mirror of internal payments queues on the bank-activity
  topic** at the internal and mixed knees' top steps, behind one bank's
  shard.
- **One bank opens about 120 to 140 accounts a second**, its openings
  taken in turn. Beyond that the API's concurrency limit turns requests
  away with a 503 at once, measuring about 150 in flight, and the API
  stays up.
- **Reads reach about 2,500 a second** on one API replica, the limit then
  measuring about 1,750 in flight and turning the rest away.
- **Onboarding slows at the verification session**, not at the party:
  every queue stayed under 20 at 80 customers a second.

## Known limitations

- **k6 shares the VM.** The load generator takes CPU from the services it
  measures, so every ceiling here is a floor for the same installation on
  dedicated nodes.
- **One bank per run.** Every bank-keyed queue — account openings, the
  bank-activity shard — is measured at one bank's share.
- **Simulated providers.** The Modulr and Zyphe simulators stand in for
  the providers, with no network between them and the adapters.
